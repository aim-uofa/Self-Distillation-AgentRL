### TASK: life_stages ###

For identifying biological life stages, apply these strategies:

1. Read the Required Stage Sequence: Identify the organism and each stage requested by the task, including their order. Distinguish stage identification from growing a new plant or comparing species lifespans; unnecessary experiments can waste the limited interaction budget.

2. Locate Relevant Organisms: Navigate to the task's specified room and inspect the biological entities there. Keep their observed names and locations, including container qualifiers where several specimens share a name. Search another plausible location only when necessary.

3. Inspect Stage Evidence: Use visible descriptions or object inspection to determine each specimen's current stage. Do not equate a species name with a particular stage, and do not assume that the name or state remains unchanged after time advances.

4. Focus on the Correct Specimen: Match the next requested stage to the corresponding observed organism and issue its precise focus command. Resolve numbered ambiguity by choosing the intended specimen, rather than selecting an arbitrary different stage.

5. Track Completion Order: After feedback confirms a selection, move to the next required stage. Record which stages have been selected to avoid repeating completed actions or skipping a necessary intermediate selection in an ordered task.

6. Wait Only for Required Development: If the task actually requires an organism to develop, allow time and reinspect its changed state. Otherwise identify existing specimens directly. Do not assume that focusing itself causes growth or guarantees task completion.
