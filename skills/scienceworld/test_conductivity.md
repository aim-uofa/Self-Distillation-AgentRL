### TASK: test_conductivity ###

For electrical conductivity experiments, apply these strategies:

1. Identify Sample and Reporting Rule: Read which object must be tested and how conductive versus non-conductive outcomes should be reported. Follow the required focus instruction, and locate the specified destination containers before performing the final classification.

2. Locate Working Components: Find a power source, wires, and a light bulb or other indicator. Inspect the available component names and terminals. Use the actual objects in this episode rather than assuming particular wire colors or bulb colors.

3. Construct a Closed Series Circuit: Connect opposite source terminals through the indicator and both terminals of the sample to make a complete loop. Include the sample in the current path; an accidental bypass tests the wire instead of the sample.

4. Check Connections Before Drawing Conclusions: A dark bulb can indicate a broken circuit or unavailable power, not necessarily an insulating sample. Verify terminal references, continuity, and any relevant activation or switch state before deciding the material is non-conductive.

5. Observe the Indicator: Allow a short simulation step if needed, then inspect whether the indicator activates. Base classification on the experimental observation, especially for an unknown substance; do not infer its identity from a previous task variation.

6. Finish the Specified Classification: Move or focus on the required object or destination according to the current task's rule. Do not hard-code blue as conductive or orange as insulating. Confirm the final action through environment feedback.
