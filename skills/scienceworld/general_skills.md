### GENERAL SKILLS ###

Here are useful strategies for completing scientific tasks in this environment:

1. Parse the Exact Goal: Identify the target object, named room, required operation, and final focus or placement condition before acting. Preserve explicit ordering such as first focus on a tool, then focus on the sample. Apply this at the start of every task.

2. Navigate Through Observed Connections: Use the room and door descriptions to plan a short route. Open a closed door before going to its destination. Do not assume teleportation is enabled or that all containers are already open. Apply this whenever changing rooms.

3. Reuse Recent Observations: Inspect a newly reached room and record relevant objects, containers, devices, and exits. Avoid repeatedly looking around when the latest feedback already supplies the needed information. Reinspect when an action changes the scene or an object cannot be found.

4. Use Exact Object References: Copy object names and, when necessary, their container or inventory qualifiers from observations. If the environment presents numbered disambiguation options, select the intended option rather than repeating the ambiguous command. Apply this after an ambiguous or failed interaction.

5. Secure Necessary Tools: Locate and acquire the instrument or movable sample needed for the next experiment. Check inventory only when possession is uncertain, and transport related items together when possible. Apply this before leaving a room with essential equipment.

6. Respect Required Focus Actions: Follow task-specific focus instructions in their stated order. A successful focus acknowledgement identifies the selected object; it does not by itself prove that measurement, transformation, or delivery has been completed. Apply this before required experiments and final selections.

7. Check Preconditions Before Retrying: A failed action should trigger a check of the object's name, location, accessibility, container state, or device activation. Fix the missing prerequisite instead of repeating an unchanged command. Apply this whenever feedback rejects an action.

8. Separate Preparation from Observation: Put materials in a suitable container, position or connect the apparatus, and activate it when needed. Then read the resulting observation or use the measuring tool to check progress. Do not confuse issuing an operation with confirming its effect.

9. Read Instance-Specific Instructions: Obtain thresholds, destination colors, recipes, and final selection rules from the current task or readable documents. Never reuse a previous episode's box color, material identity, or numerical result as an answer. Apply this to conditional and scientific classification tasks.

10. Finish the Actual Requirement: Complete the specified placement, focus sequence, or observed transformation after obtaining evidence. Avoid extra exploration once the environment finishes the task, and use short waits only when an ongoing physical process needs time to advance.
